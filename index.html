>> Spoiler Alert: This will have to sound a bit like bragging. But honestly, I'm really just excited about what I do. <<

My name is Sascha. I grew up in Germany first. And then a second time around the world. Creativity is what I live for. Since the beginning of it all, it has driven my evolution. It's also closely linked to being an entrepreneur and self-starter.

Currently I am the Chief Creative Officer and agency co-lead of BBH Asia Pacific. We're based in Singapore. My wife is from Hong Kong and my three kids will rule the world rule my world.

I've led the global launch of Samsung's first Ai phone in 2024 reversing a decade of sales decline. Which was followed by the successful launch of Samsung's flagship phone S25 Ultra the year after.

Having worked in multiple markets with people from all cultural backgrounds has given me a super power to really understand the world and its cultural differences.

This helped me lead BBH Singapore to become a global agency that now does global campaigns from the little red dot: For Samsung's TV business, its brand, global health platform and first-ever Watch Ultra.

But it's not all Samsung. I've led the evolution of the British heritage brand Mulberry out of Asia including the launch of their first-ever carbon neutral collection. We even orchestrated the global relaunch of Sweden's Absolut and tried to meddle with the American elections.

While I love the complexities and scale of big global launches, as a creative, I get excited about hacking things, doing things differently and experimenting with what's possible to make what we do more exciting and effective. I do everything I can to foster an environment where that's possible. The proof's in the pudding:

A fake commercial led to a telco's best product launch with the Premiere League Fan Test. Unbranding the world's most iconic can got people in the Middle East to rethink Coca-Cola.

And while I started my career in film shooting crime series for German TV, now I like to also push narratives from simple linear stories to interactive experiences. When TikTok was new and just 6 seconds, we launched an interactive RomCom on the platform that had billions permutations. We staged a bounty hunt on leading gaming titles that hijacked streamers' feeds for Codashop. And during Covid we developed our own running app that was also an entertainment platform. The BBC loved it when they flew in to test.

Before Fandom became a "thing" I led the team behind some of Riot Games' most iconic work. The physical manifestation of the famous power up Honeyfruit as well as the first-ever gamification of a Youtube Live Stream to launch the mobile version of the world's most played PC game: League of Legends. Needless to say we topped the App Store in all markets. The rest is now lore.

I put Dr. Strange on the Sphere in Las Vegas for Samsung and beer into a shoe for Heineken leading to their most successful new product launch in 160 years of company history. It made other brand units within the company jealous and so we developed the tropic's first puffer jacket the year after for Tiger Beer. I love it when our work changes company culture.

When Wendy Clarke was the global CMO of Coca-Cola, one day, Nestle's CEO posted a video I directed on her Facebook page. It was the company's first global content marketing idea that had just gone viral. She hadn't seen it. Needless to say it made everyone nervous at first. But when Apple's Chief Evangelist Guy Kawasaki praised the Social Media Guard as a product everyone should buy, everyone was chill again. Of course, people got promoted.

Google's CEO Sundai Pichar called our Circle to Search campaign the best partner marketing Google has ever done. The Iranian censorship board however didn't like our campaign for Japanese energy drink Vitaene C. It got banned! And became so popular in culture that sales went through the roof and the brand expanded. I bagged my first director award for it too. Yes, I still direct from time to time. Believe it or not, I also worked in the marketing department for Snickers for a while… Too many stories for such a tiny website.

I make a point to see creatives as creators. The former is hypothetical and just comes up with ideas which is the easy part. The second makes them happen – much harder. Upskilling our production department and fostering a maker spirit now allows the agency to produce things that wouldn't be possible otherwise. Or say possible within budget and time frame. We produced Singapore's first legal chewing gum. We turned our employee handbook into a piece of wearable fashion. We made our agency manifesto a soap. And to celebrate the centenary of Coca-Cola's Contour bottle, I tracked down the family of its inventor and got access to an interview his son did with him before he died. It led to this beautiful piece of design that honoured the story of the most iconic packaging in history.

A Nike hair tie we made for Alex Eala for her Wimbledon debut put her home nation (exclusively Basketball fanatics) into a Tennis craze. For McDonald's we democratised hype drops and created a downloadable and printable idea: Gamifries. No other idea organically reached so many gamers before. When culture can make our ideas their own, we've won. Whether for a good cause like press freedom or delicious soda.

In 2013, my arguable most impactful campaign truly changed culture around the world and put gender equality back on the map. I warned you that this would sound like bragging. UNWomen's first global campaign eventually won the Middle East's first Titanium Lion in Cannes.

The work from me and the teams won pretty much every award in every category in every major award show including 9 Grand Prix. But don't we all have these lists?

I get more excited about doing things others don't. Like launching an original song with 90's pop band The Calling for an insurance company. Or a horror movie to sell travel insurance. Will Smith once even optioned an original movie idea we created. (That was before the slap).

I believe that creativity is not a department. It's a lived culture.

When the work is driven by creatives only – in my experience – it's usually scam or for awards. Real change only happens when everyone's contributing.

I believe in people. And everyone's unique contribution. The more different people are the better.

And I believe that ego, politics and assumptions are the three horsemen of any organisation's apocalypse.

Having worked in difficult environments and markets has taught me to be resourceful and allowed me to develop a leadership style that adapts to the circumstances. I enjoy leading big teams and build company culture. But I get equally excited about running a nimble team where we're all in the trenches.

Aside from work, I nurture a few hobbies. I publish comedic music under Sam Not Sam or write joke books for kids.

So what is it that I haven't done yet? Why don't you get in touch and we find out together?
